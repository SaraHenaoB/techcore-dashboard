# Findings

1. From 2017 to 2024, total sales remained stable within a range of 40,275 – 42,745 million pesos; however, for 2025 there is a significant reduction of 66.1% compared to the previous year. Even with the year unfinished, at least 3,527M in sales must be secured in each of the last 4 months, including the last registered month, which is September.

2. The purchasing trend persists. The leading brands since sales began in 2014 are Lenovo and HP with 2,000 units sold each, while the least requested are MSI and Razer, with 101 and 45 units sold respectively in 2024. Additionally, reported profits are around 850 and 1,000 million for top brands, compared to 70 and 34 million for the latter.

3. In the last 3 years, the age group with the greatest impact on sales is 26–40 years, as most individuals in this range are professionally established with stable income. Furthermore, considering the pandemic and technological innovation, they are more willing to invest in tech.

4. Within the previous category, credit card usage is observed as the preferred payment method, generating 20,135M in sales—a difference of 10,600M compared to the second most used method, debit card.

5. The cities with the highest market share are Medellín and Bogotá, representing 40.7% and 30.6% of sales share respectively. This trend holds even when filtered exclusively for the 26–40 age group and credit card payment method.

# Conclusions and Recommendations for TechCore

1. For the year 2025, a monthly billing of at least 3,527M must be guaranteed for each of the remaining 4 months to maintain the trend of previous years and avoid a financial deficit.

2. Given that the brands with the highest historical sales are Lenovo and HP, it is recommended to run promotions where purchasing a Lenovo or HP product yields a discount (e.g., 30%, 50%, or 100%, determined by TechCore) on MSI and Razer products. This will incentivize purchases of lower-turnover items.

3. Execute targeted marketing aimed at the 26–40 age bracket, as they demonstrate the highest willingness to invest in tech and possess the purchasing power to do so. Additionally, a trade-in program could be introduced allowing customers to purchase the latest Lenovo or HP device by turning in their current device of the same brand as partial payment.

4. Evaluate the feasibility of a rewards point system for purchases made with credit cards. Upon reaching specific point thresholds, customers could redeem benefits from a curated catalog (determined by TechCore), such as 24/7 product support, 1-to-2-year extended warranties on best-selling products like Lenovo, or discounts on lower-turnover items up to 100% off to receive them "for free."

5. Initiate these recommendations in the city of Medellín—which holds the largest market share—and measure the impact on overall sales and units sold of lower-turnover products. If results are positive and significant, roll out the strategy to the next key city, Bogotá.
